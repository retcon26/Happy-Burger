# Happy Burger

A burger stand sim for the PSP, in the spirit of Citizen Burger Disorder, by Retcon. Version **0.1.0**.

It runs on the **Retcon's Mod engine**: this repository is a fork of
[retcons-mod](https://github.com/retcon26/retcons-mod) 1.14.1 (its history included), with the game written as a
Lua gamemode on top and **all its own content**: low-poly models, characters, a map, sounds and UI, generated by
[tools/burgerkit](tools/burgerkit). It needs nothing from any other game.
[docs/ENGINE.md](docs/ENGINE.md) is Retcon's Mod's own README, and everything in it still applies.

## The game

You run the kitchen. Customers come in off the street and up to the counter, one per window, and order. The ticket at the
top of the screen shows what they want and how much patience they have left.

- **Burgers are cooked and built.** Use the fridge for a raw patty (pink) and carry it to the grill: it
  browns as it cooks, then goes overdone (half pay) and burnt (black). Use the bun rack for a bottom and a top
  bun, and the bins for cheese, lettuce and tomato. On a cutting board, put a bottom bun, the toppings the
  ticket asks for, the patty and a top bun: they become the burger. A raw or burnt patty makes a raw or burnt
  burger, and the wrong toppings get it sent back. There are two boards, so two cooks can build at once.
- **Hot dogs** (from day 3): grill a sausage from the freezer, then put it and a hot dog bun on a board.
- **Fries** (from day 2): Use the fryer and wait five seconds for a carton to pop out beside it.
- **Soda:** Use the soda machine.
- **Serve** by putting each meal on the yellow tray in front of the customer who ordered it. You can throw
  it there too, but a burger that hits a customer makes them storm off. Raw, burnt, wrong or half-built food
  bounces back and costs patience. Tickets show each burger's toppings as coloured chips: C cheese, L
  lettuce, T tomato. The bin takes mistakes, and when there's too much food out the oldest goes off.
- **Pay** is the menu price ($1 more per topping) plus a tip for speed, scaled by your star rating. Happy customers raise it;
  walkouts, hits and (please don't) killing a customer lower it. At zero stars you're shut down.
- **Days** get longer and busier, with bigger orders. Make the day's quota (on Medium, $20 on day 1 and $12
  more each day) or you're fired and start again at day 1. From day 4 **mingebags** come in: impatient, and when they're kept
  waiting they go on a rampage. From day 5 **VIPs** (the G-Man, Dr. Breen) tip double but wait less.

### Difficulty

**Start Shift** asks how hard:

- **Tutorial:** a guided first shift. It walks you through a cheeseburger one step at a time (fridge, grill,
  buns, cheese, the board, the tray), then a soda, waiting for you to do each step. If the patty burns, it
  tells you to bin it and start again. Then your first Easy shift starts.
- **Easy:** patient customers, small orders, few toppings, a grill that's slow to burn; hot dogs from day
  4, no mingebags until day 6.
- **Medium:** the full shift: toppings, fries from day 2, hot dogs from day 3, mingebags from day 4, VIPs
  from day 5.
- **Hard:** shorter tempers, more toppings, higher quotas, a grill that burns sooner; everything arrives
  early; walkouts cost more stars. Pay is 25% more.
- **Extreme Burgers:** everything from day one, customers who run in and won't wait, a grill that burns in a
  blink, and a quota to match. Pay is 50% more.

The levels were tuned with a simulated, human-paced cook (one pair of hands, a few seconds to fetch and
carry each thing). An average player lasts about 8 days on Easy, 5 on Medium, 3 to 4 on Hard and 1 on Extreme
Burgers; a quick one about 3 on Extreme. Your last pick is remembered.

Play alone, or host and up to three friends join as staff, sharing the till (hosting asks how hard too).

### Controls

| Action | PSP |
|---|---|
| Walk / look | Analog stick / face buttons |
| Grab: take from a station (fridge, buns, bins, fryer, soda machine), pick food up, put it down | R |
| Throw what you're holding | L |
| Jump (hands empty) | L |
| Quick chat / talk (co-op) | Hold D-pad Down / Up |
| Staff list | Hold Select |
| Pause | Start |

Look at anything in the kitchen and the bottom of the screen says what it is: a patty's doneness, or what a
station does.

### The burger joint

The game has its own map, made for it: a kitchen behind a long counter, a dining room with booths, and the
street outside, where customers come in through the front door. **Start Shift** on the title screen goes
straight there, and **Play Together** hosts a shift for up to three friends or joins one. Options has the
detail settings (draw distance, detail distance, physics quality, target FPS) and the basics.

## What's changed from the engine

- **`NPC:SetGoal(pos, yaw, run)`, `NPC:AtGoal()`, `NPC:ClearGoal()`:** a script walks an NPC somewhere (round
  walls on maps with a node graph) and has it stand facing a way, with its own AI off meanwhile. It's how
  customers queue at the counter ([psp/src/npc.c](psp/src/npc.c), [docs/LUA.md](docs/LUA.md)).
- **The gamemode:** [psp/lua/gamemodes/burger.lua](psp/lua/gamemodes/burger.lua), built into the EBOOT, and New
  Game's default.
- **Its own game:** the XMB title, `PSP/GAME/HappyBurger`, screenshots in `PSP/PHOTO/HappyBurger`, the title
  screen and credits. Options > Updates looks for releases of `retcon26/happy-burger`, not Retcon's Mod's (whose
  EBOOT would replace this game). Until that repository has a release, the check just fails harmlessly.

- **Retcon's Mod's leftovers gone from the menus:** no Free Play (the sandbox), Workshop or Achievements;
  Options keeps just the basics and the detail settings; R grabs from stations (`GM.HandsUse`). The engine's
  sandbox and other gamemodes are still in the code, just not on the menus.

## Building

Everything is original and made from code, so nothing needs converting from other games:

- **The data** (2.8 MB): the low-poly props and characters, their animations, the map, the sounds and the
  UI, made by [tools/burgerkit](tools/burgerkit). Needs Python 3 with numpy and Pillow:

  ```bash
  python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install numpy pillow
  .venv/bin/python -m tools.burgerkit
  ```

  It writes `psp/data` (and the XMB's `psp/ICON0.PNG` and `PIC1.PNG`). `--wavs DIR` also saves every sound
  as a .wav to listen to.
- **The EBOOT**: the PSP SDK ([pspdev](https://github.com/pspdev/pspdev)) in `~/pspdev`. On a Mac, its
  macOS build needs Homebrew's `libmpc`, `mpfr` and `gmp`. Then:

  ```bash
  bash psp/build.sh
  ```

**To play:** copy `psp/EBOOT.PBP` and `psp/data` into `PSP/GAME/HappyBurger/` on the memory stick, or in
PPSSPP use File > Load on `psp/EBOOT.PBP` (the `data` folder must sit next to it).

All of the game's art, models, animation, sound and the map are its own, generated by tools/burgerkit. The
font is Pillow's Aileron (public domain). The engine is Retcon's Mod's.
